🔑 Google App Password & SMTP Guide
Follow these 3 essential steps to get flawless Google SMTP & IMAP authentication
Why does Google require an App Password?
Google protects your primary password by requiring a dedicated 16-letter App Password for third-party email tools.

Step 1: Enable IMAP in Gmail Settings

  1. Open mail.google.com while logged into your sender account.
  2. Click the Settings Gear ⚙️ (top right) → click See all settings.
  3. Go to the Forwarding and POP/IMAP tab.
  4. Under IMAP access, select Enable IMAP.
  5. Scroll to the bottom and click Save Changes.

Step 2: Check 2-Step Verification

Visit myaccount.google.com/security and confirm that 2-Step Verification is ON for your sender account.

Step 3: Generate a 16-Letter App Password

  1. Go directly to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords.
  2. Type MailFlow into the app name field and click Create.
  3. Copy the 16-character code (e.g. abcd efgh ijkl mnop) and paste it into MailFlow.

            🛡️ Why Cold Emails Land in Spam & How to Fix It
          

          

            The 5 Critical Rules for Email Deliverability & Reputation on Gmail & Outlook
          

        

        
      


      

        
        
        

          

            🚫 Trap 1: Fake Support Ticket Numbers (Critical Spam Trigger)
            Highest Risk
          

          

            What triggers it: Subjects like Ticket IRN #1240530: Wix website enhancement... or Ticket #4928.

            Why it goes to Spam: Google and Outlook use machine learning to detect unsolicited emails pretending to be existing customer support tickets or invoices. Because your free @gmail.com address has no authenticated Zendesk/Freshdesk records and no prior email thread history, Google flags it as Deceptive Phishing and dumps it into Spam immediately!
          

          

            ✓ The Fix: Use short, conversational human subjects such as:
            
    
              
  • quick question
    • 
              
  • quick question for {{name}}
    • 
              
  • website feedback
    • 
              
  • idea for your business
    • 
            

          

        


        
        

          

            ⚠️ Trap 2: Testing to Your Own Email Address
            Common Mistake
          

          

            What happens: You send a test email from yourname@gmail.com to yourname@gmail.com.

            Why it goes to Spam: Google's incoming mail server treats emails sent from an external SMTP client to the exact same account as spoofing/internal forgery, placing it in Spam with the warning: "Be careful with this message... Google couldn't verify that it was actually sent by you."
          

          

            ✓ The Fix: When testing, always send to a different recipient email address (e.g. your secondary email, Yahoo, Outlook, or test on mail-tester.com).
          

        


        
        

          

            🏷️ Trap 3: Claiming Brand Affiliation from @gmail.com
            Reputation Rule
          

          

            Why it triggers filters: Sending from a free @gmail.com while using names like "Wix Partner" or "Google Specialist" triggers brand protection heuristics.
          

          

            ✓ The Fix: Use your real human name or company name in Display Name (e.g. Alexa Smith or Alexa | Web Consultant).
          

        


        
        

          

            🛡️ Secret 4: Pure Stealth Plain-Text Mode
            Active by Default
          

          

            MailFlow sends in Stealth Plain-Text Mode by default. This strips all HTML containers, tables, and tracking pixels. The recipient's email provider sees a 100% authentic personal email sent from a human inbox, bypassing both Spam and the Promotions tab directly into Primary Inbox.
          

        


        
        

          

            ⏱️ Secret 5: Humanized Jitter Delay & Spintax Rotation
            Active by Default
          

          

            Keep the 15s–20s interval and use Spintax ({Hello|Hi|Hey}) across your 5 message variants. This ensures no two emails share identical hashes, completely avoiding Google's bulk pattern filters.
